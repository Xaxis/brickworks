# Hidden colours

Big LEGO® sets are often coloured inside in a way they never are outside: a grey
Star Destroyer is red, yellow and blue under its plates, the Eiffel Tower's core is
red one way and yellow the other. The colours are there for the person building,
and gone once the model is finished. This is what the practice is, measured over
the real sets we have, and how Brickworks does the same: Tools, Colour the hidden
parts…, and `colour_hidden_parts` over MCP.

Sources are linked where they are used. **[V]** marks a page read and quoted
exactly; **[C]** a reader's comment or a forum post, not the LEGO Group; **[I]** our
own inference. Reddit could not be read.

## What LEGO does, and why

**The reason, from a designer.** A former LEGO designer, writing on Brickset in
April 2024 ([Bright coloured filler bricks: what's up with that?](https://brickset.com/article/107900/bright-coloured-filler-bricks-what-s-up-with-that)):

- "Lowering the time to find bricks is one of the things that gets scrutinised
  intensely when designing a LEGO model." [V]
- His example is a Star Wars set "where there are 99% grey wing plates": one bright
  brick among them is found at once. It is "an opportunity to use a brick that
  contrasts well with everything else in that bag, so it stands out." [V]
- "All this is backed up by extensive user research and observation, with both
  kids and adults." In the comments: "the number one reason is build flow." [V]
- Not cost: "If that were the case, every filler colour would be black", and
  "literally zero consideration is given to Bricklink prices." [V]
- How the colour is chosen: designers "can see what bricks in what colours are
  already in production", and "wouldn't waste a colour change on a filler
  element." [V]

So: a colour already made in that mould, chosen to stand out from the rest of
that bag, in a place that will be covered. Everything below is that rule applied.

So it comes in three shapes, all of which Brickworks offers:

- **by section or stage**: the colour tells you which part of the build you are in
  (Razor Crest, Titanic, Colosseum, the Star Destroyer);
- **by side or axis**: two colours for two halves that would otherwise look the same
  (Eiffel Tower north–south against east–west, DeLorean front against back, a
  ship's port and starboard [C, [31109](https://brickset.com/article/101719)]);
- **only where it ends up hidden**: never on a face anyone sees.

No source was found, either way, for 75192 Millennium Falcon, 75313 AT-AT, 10316
Rivendell, 10333 Barad-dûr or the modular buildings 10255, 10297, 10312 and 10326. The
measured section below looks at the ones we have files for. The colours named are
red, blue, yellow, white, green and pink, and once bright orange; none of the
reviews names lime, azure, magenta or lavender as typical, though New Elementary
picks azure as the stock example ("Mediaeval master builders didn't hide azure
blocks inside their edifices", [21061 review](https://www.newelementary.com/2024/05/lego-architecture-review-21061-notre.html)). [V]

## When it is not done, and what people say

- **Accurate on purpose.** 21061 Notre-Dame is built in the cathedral's own order,
  which "results in the model featuring none of the hidden, brightly coloured parts
  so common in LEGO sets" (New Elementary, above). 10283 Space Shuttle Discovery:
  "The inside colors of the model are olive green because the internal structure of
  the shuttle was green" ([collectSPACE](https://www.collectspace.com/news/news-040121a-lego-space-shuttle-discovery-designer-interview.html)). [V]
- **It shows through gaps.** On the Star Destroyer, "you can see a lot of the
  multicoulour interior through the various gaps" [C]; 10333's "blue pin is quite
  conspicuous from behind" ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/110009/review-10333-barad-dur)) [V];
  in the 42223 GT40, "I don't see a reason for the usage of this bright colour in
  such a dark model" ([New Elementary](https://www.newelementary.com/2025/12/review-42223-1966-ford-gt40-mkii-from.html)) [V].
  Collectors who keep the parts for their own builds want grey or black inside
  ("interior color vomit", [Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/82137)) [C], and
  some refuse such sets [C].
- **Builders of their own models** ([Eurobricks, 2018](https://www.eurobricks.com/forum/forums/topic/163537-opinions-on-instructions-coloring-of-internal-parts/)) [C]:
  one colour for everything hidden "makes a complex internal build into one large
  yellow blob, which would be hard to decipher"; colour by part type "looks messy";
  one builder uses red, yellow and blue in layers, "just like TLG does it".
- **Colour blindness.** A colour-blind ten-year-old "really appreciated the use of
  brightly contrasting interior bricks" ([The Brothers Brick, 41843](https://www.brothers-brick.com/2025/09/10/lego-41843-family-christmas-tree-a-holiday-tradition-in-the-making-review/)) [V]
  — and the commonest kind cannot tell red from green, so those two should never be
  the only pair. [I]
- **Not the old rainbow.** "Rainbow warrior" builds ([SEALUG](https://sealug.org/?p=532)) [V]
  and pre-2005 sets with visible odd colours came from a narrow palette and stock
  ([Eurobricks](https://www.eurobricks.com/forum/forums/topic/106213-pre-2005-star-wars-lego-why-did-the-designers-use-random-colours)) [C].
  That is visible and accidental; this is hidden and deliberate. The measured
  section tells the two apart by whether the odd colour is ever seen.

## Measured in real sets

Every file of the LDraw model repository we have (vendor/omr, 1,576 files of
1,470 sets) opened as File, Open opens it and measured by the same code the
feature uses (`src/dev/hidden_colours_audit.gd`, four shards on the GPU, about
40 minutes), then added up by `tools/hidden_colours_audit.py`. Hidden here is
"on a table": seen from no side but from below, as a displayed model is looked
at. A set's **accent colours** are the ones it uses mostly where nobody sees
them: a vivid plain colour (CIE chroma 28 or more and lightness 33 or more, so
browns, dark reds and greys are filler, not marks) with three or more hidden
parts and at least two of every three of its parts hidden. The fan-made files
follow the instructions part for part, colours included. The repository is
measured from 48 sides, one picture each, at up to 1,024 pixels, coarser than
the app's 128 sides drawn twice at 2,048; the GPU (Forward+) and software
(Compatibility) renderers agree within a part or two (10182: 258 and 256
hidden; 10276: 304 and 303).

1,576 sets of the LDraw model repository, 687,038 parts, each measured from 48 sides at up to 1,024 pixels (a pixel 1 LDU on a small set, about 3 on the largest). Hidden on a table, seen from no side but underneath: 58,276 (8.5%). Hidden from underneath too: 43,668 (6.4%).

Of the hidden parts, 3,549 (6.1%) are in a colour the set uses mostly inside, vivid and at least two thirds hidden (an **accent**). 1,258 (2.2%) are in a colour no seen part of the set has at all, 869 of those vivid. 68 sets (4.3%) have five or more accent parts.

### What it says

- **It is a big-set, recent practice.** Fewer than 1 set in 100 under 200 parts
  marks its inside; a fifth of the sets over 1,000 parts do. Of the sets of 500
  parts and more, none before 1990, 3% in the 1990s, 19% in 2015–19 and 36% since
  2020. The early-2000s bump is a handful of big sets built round a blue core.
- **Blue, yellow, red and green**, in that order (41, 33, 28 and 20 sets), then a few modern brights (Dark
  Turquoise, Bright Pink, Lime, Orange, Medium Azure in six or seven sets each).
  That is what the reviews name: the Colosseum's blue, red and yellow, the
  Titanic's five colours, the Saturn V's red, yellow and green all come out of the
  files. Brickworks offers its colours in this measured order.
- **LEGO marks; it does not paint the whole core.** Only 6.1% of hidden parts are
  in an accent colour, and only 2.2% are in a colour nothing outside has at all:
  the accent colour is very often also used, sparingly, somewhere seen. Brickworks
  colours every hidden part that can take its group's colour, and keeps every
  colour seen outside out of the inside, which is stricter on both counts. A
  person who wants LEGO's lighter touch can apply it to one stage and not the rest.
- **By stage of the build more than by section.** An accent colour's parts are
  packed into a stretch of the build (the middle half of them span 22% of it,
  against 41% for hidden parts in general): a bag's worth. They keep to one
  sub-model more than chance too (74% against 55%), but less strongly. So "by
  build stage" is the closest to what LEGO does; "by section" is the natural
  choice for a model a designer built in named sections.
- **Halves are almost never told apart by colour.** Of 6,646 mirror pairs of
  hidden parts left and right, 99% are the same colour, and only 3 pairs (one set)
  are two different accents; front and back, 11 pairs in 3 sets. The Eiffel
  Tower and the DeLorean are the exceptions the reviews remember. Brickworks offers
  left and right, and front and back, for the symmetrical build that needs it, and
  never as a default.
- **Technic does it by function instead.** The flagships 42083, 42056 and 42115
  have no accent colours to speak of (four Bright Green parts in 42115): their
  colours say what a pin or an axle is, and their stages are numbered boxes.
- **The measure and LEGO agree on what "hidden" is.** Of the parts in the sets'
  accent colours, the measure calls 81% hidden on a table, and 67% hidden from
  underneath too: about one in six of LEGO's hidden accents can be seen from below
  once the model is picked up, and a fifth of them glimpsed on a table, through a
  gap, which is what the reviews complain of. So Brickworks' default, "seen from
  underneath too", is stricter than LEGO; turned off, it measures as LEGO places.


## On our models and real sets

`src/dev/hidden_colours_pictures.gd` on the GPU, the final code: each model
opened as File, Open opens it, measured (128 sides, twice each, up to 2,048
pixels), planned, applied as the panel's Apply does, then drawn before and after
from eight sides, three of which the measure never looks from (one of those from
below, left out for a display model), and compared pixel for pixel. Before and
after are drawn in one world with only the colours changed
(`SeenParts.pictures`): two worlds built alike draw in their own order, and on
the seams where two parts' faces lie flush that order decides which face shows;
compared across two worlds, the Colosseum's base "differed" by 122 pixels with

Every model's outside is the same pixel for pixel from every side compared, and
no copy of a sub-assembly was rebuilt (`Assemblies.unlike_copies` put back 16 to
60 parts a set whose copies would otherwise no longer have matched). On the
sets, LEGO's own accents are kept ("coded": the Colosseum's 36), and many hidden
parts keep their colour because they were never made in their stage's colour
("not made") or the catalogue does not know what they were made in. Each
model's pictures are in `shots/hidden_colours/<file>/`: `sheet.png` has the
outside above and the inside, cut open where most of the change is, below,
before on the left. On a GPU a model takes 20 seconds (Café Corner) to two and a
half minutes (the Colosseum).

Two faults were found this way and fixed: the measure drew both sides of every
face where the app's plastic culls back faces (it now culls as the plastic does),
and a symmetrical sub-assembly's copies could fit one another a different way
after colouring, so Saturn V had three copies rebuilt (now put back before the
step is kept).

`src/dev/hidden_colours_probe.gd` holds the rest in the suite: a closed box's
three inner bricks found exactly, a gap and a clear window seen through, the
castle through the panel's own Apply (outside the same from eight sides, one
Undo), a real set refused without a yes, and the booklet's marks.

## How Brickworks does it

Tools, Colour the hidden parts… (`src/ui/hidden_colours_panel.gd`), and
`colour_hidden_parts` over MCP, which runs the same code.

1. **Find the parts nobody sees, by measuring.** `SeenParts`
   (`src/render/seen_parts.gd`) draws the model from 128 sides — the 26 of a cube and
   102 more spread evenly over the sphere, underneath included — each twice, half a
   pixel apart, with no perspective, culled as the app's plastic is,
   every part a flat colour that is its own number (`seen_codes.gdshader`), at a pixel
   of 1 LDU or as fine as 2,048 pixels allow, and reads every number back. A part in
   none of the pictures is hidden; nothing else is. It is a sample: a part seen only
   through a crack narrower than a pixel, from a side between those drawn, could be
   missed, and the comparison above, from sides never drawn, found none on seven
   models. See-through parts are left out of
   the pictures, so what is behind a window is seen. "Seen from underneath too" is on
   by default (a model is picked up); off, it measures as a display model is looked at.
2. **Group them** by section (the booklet's sections: the assemblies a design named, a
   sub-model, a region of a big model), by build stage (the booklet's steps cut into
   stages of about 300 parts, as bags are; the person can set how many), or by half
   (left and right, front and back).
3. **One colour a group.** From the colours real sets put inside, in the order they use
   them (blue, yellow, red, green, then the modern brights), and the other strong
   colours in production; each at least 22 CIE76 units from every colour
   seen on the outside and 20 from the other groups', in production
   (`PartLibrary.is_current`); the largest group chooses first, the colour most of its
   hidden parts were made in. Each part takes the group's colour only if the catalogue
   says that part was made in it (`PartInfo.colors`, preferring `colors_recent`), else
   a near colour no other group has, else it keeps its own.
4. **Never what is seen**, nor: see-through, chrome, rubber and other finishes;
   printed and stickered parts; minifigures; locked parts; parts whose colours nobody
   knows; a hidden part already in a vivid colour nothing outside has (somebody coded
   it: LEGO, or the person); and any part of a copied sub-assembly unless its
   counterpart in every copy is hidden and in the same group, because copies are kept
   alike (`Assemblies.after_edit`) and colouring one would colour the others.
5. **A preview, then one step.** The panel shows the inside cut open through the
   middle as it is and as it would be, a chip per group, and what is kept and why.
   Apply is one step to undo. The choice goes with the model as a line LDraw readers
   pass over (`0 !BRICKWORKS HIDDEN_COLOURS {...}`).
6. **Sets and the person's own colours.** A model opened from the LDraw model
   repository knows the set it is (`ModelStore.from_set`, kept when saved): the panel
   says so and Apply waits for a tick, and over MCP `set_agreed=true` is needed on top
   of `agreed=true`. Over MCP nothing is applied without `agreed=true`, the person's
   yes; the designer only offers it, on the card of a big model built in sections and
   at the end of a session's design, never by default.
7. **The booklet** draws the model as it is coloured, so the steps and callouts show
   the colours; and each colour is said where its group starts: in the section's banner
   ("inside: Bright Green"), else under the number of the step its first part goes in,
   and in the corner of a callout built with it.
