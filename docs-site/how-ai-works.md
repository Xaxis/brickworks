What an AI puts on your baseplate is real parts that fit and hold, checked as yours are. This page says what the AI works with, what the app checks, and how to ask for what you mean. Whichever AI designs, your own AI app through the connector, or Claude or another AI on your own key, it builds the same way and is held to the same checks.

## The same parts, the same checks {#same-checks}

An AI does not draw a model; it proposes parts, and Brickworks decides. Every design goes through the checks a part you place by hand does, and more:

- **Real parts only**, found by search in the catalogue, in colours each was really made in.
- **Each part where it fits**, on studs, pins, axles, clips and hinges, by its connectors, never passing into another.
- **The check by real shapes**: finished, every part is held by a stud, a joint or what catches it, judged by the parts' exact shapes.
- **It stands**: balance and the strength of each joint, as [Check stability](/docs/joints-and-mechanisms#stability) weighs them.

A design that fails is sent back with what failed, part by part, and the AI fixes it before it is kept. What lands on your baseplate is one step of Undo.

## How it builds well {#how}

Brickworks hands the AI what a set designer knows:

- **Patterns** for shapes: a curve, a taper, a dome, a wall with windows, said as a pattern and laid in real parts.
- **How real sets build it**: pieces of real sets found by what they are (a wheel arch, a roof ridge, a minifigure's chair) with pictures of real sets of the kind.
- **Worked constructions**: hinged panels, walls built sideways with their studs facing out, shells of hinged plates turned any way.
- **Figures on joints**: people, animals and heads laid on joints that turn and hold.
- **Its own best work**: the earlier design of the nearest subject that judges liked best, and what they faulted in it.
- **A surface pass** once the shape stands: open studs tiled, slopes stepped, hard edges rounded, and small detail added where a real set has it.

## It sees what you see {#sees}

![The red car as the AI sees it, from a corner, with two rulers along its base, x and z, numbered in studs, so the AI can read where things are off the picture.](/docs/img/how-ai-works-view.webp "What the AI sees of its own work: the model, with rulers in studs.")

The AI looks at its work in pictures with rulers in studs along two edges of the base, so it can read where things are off them rather than guess. It can look from any side, from up to six sides in one picture, with chosen parts picked out or the parts in front made see-through, at the model as built to a step, or at a page of the booklet as it prints; and it can ask what you are looking at: the step you are on, the part in your hand, what you have selected. Looking changes nothing. Then every new design gets [fresh eyes](/docs/fresh-eyes), a look at it as a stranger sees it.

## Never limited {#never-limited}

AI is never limited, on any plan. You bring your own AI, so Brickworks never meters it or pays for it: whatever you ask of it, its maker bills your plan or key, never Brickworks. What each plan includes is on [Plans and billing](/docs/plans).

## Tips {#tips}

- **Say the size**: *a minifigure-scale house, 16 by 16 studs*, *a Technic car about 30 cm long*.
- **Say the subject and its parts**: *a lighthouse with a red and white tower, a gallery round the lamp and a keeper's cottage*.
- **Sketch first** when you are not sure you have said what you mean: three quick sketches of the overall shape, then the one you pick.
- **Change a part, not the whole**: select it and [Ask Claude about this…](/docs/ai#ask), so nothing else moves.
- **Build together**: the AI and your hands can work on one model; each change is its own undo step.
- **Own the result**: you can change any part by hand, and what you share is yours, credited to you.
