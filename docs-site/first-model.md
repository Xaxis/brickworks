This walks through one small model, from an empty baseplate to a link you can send, in about ten minutes. Open [brickworks.diy/app](/app) and sign in; the window looks like this.

![The Brickworks window on a first visit, a red car on the baseplate. Numbered outlines mark 1, the menus along the top; 2, the toolbar; 3, the parts bin on the left; 4, the view cube; 5, Build your first model's offer over the model.](/docs/img/first-model-window.webp "The window: 1 menus, 2 toolbar, 3 parts bin, 4 view cube, 5 Build your first model.")

> [!TIP]
> Lost? Press [[Ctrl+/]] (**Find a command…**, in the Brickworks menu at the far left) and type what you want to do: *save*, *mirror*, *steps*. Each row says its key and its menu, and [[Enter]] runs it.

## 1. Take the tour, or skip it {#tour}

A first visit offers **Build your first model** along the top of the model: seven short steps, each done in the app, not read about. **Start** gives you a baseplate of your own (the example car comes off; a model of yours is kept, and **File › New** asks first if it is not saved). Each step says one thing, frames what to use and names your key for it, and moves on when the app sees it done, whichever way you do it: put a part down, turn it, colour it, move it, copy it, turn the view, and save.

**Skip** ends it at any step. The offer also goes by itself once you put three parts down by hand or open a model. **Help › Take the tour** brings it back.

## 2. Put a part down {#place}

Click a part in the **parts bin** on the left, or press [[/]], type its name and click it: it is now *in hand* and follows the pointer over the baseplate, snapping to the studs. Click to put it down. It stays in hand, so each click puts down another. Press [[Esc]] or right-click to let go, and [[Q]] to take the last part back.

![An empty green baseplate with a red 2 x 4 brick in hand, drawn where it will go under the pointer.](/docs/img/first-model-in-hand.webp "A part in hand follows the pointer and snaps to the studs; a click puts it down.")

- [[R]] turns the part in hand a quarter turn; [[T]] tips it onto its side.
- [[[]] and [[]]] step through colours; [[Shift+C]] asks for a colour by name or number.

More ways to place a part, exactly or sideways: [Placing parts](/docs/placing-parts).

## 3. Change it {#change}

Click a part to select it. Drag it to carry it somewhere else, press [[Delete]] to take it away, [[C]] to paint it in the current colour, or [[Ctrl+D]] to duplicate it. [[Ctrl+Z]] undoes anything, and **Edit › History** goes back several steps at once. See [Moving and changing parts](/docs/changing-parts).

## 4. Make a step {#step}

Press [[E]] when a stage is done: everything put down since the last step becomes one build step. [[B]] opens the timeline to see them in order. The booklet and the step player on your model's page follow these steps. See [Instructions and booklets](/docs/instructions).

## 5. Save it {#save}

Press [[Ctrl+S]]. The first save asks what to call the model. It is kept in this browser and with your account, so **File › Open** finds it anywhere you sign in. **File › Versions…** keeps its last 20 saves.

## 6. Check it holds {#check}

**Tools › Check stability** ([[Alt+K]]) shows whether the model would stand and which joints would give way. A red part is one that would come loose. See [Joints and mechanisms](/docs/joints-and-mechanisms#stability).

## 7. Share it {#share}

**File › Share…** publishes the model with a picture, a name and a kind. You get a link anyone can open in 3D, step through and build, with no account. See [Sharing a model](/docs/sharing).

> [!TIP]
> Want an AI to build the first draft? Turn on the assistant (**Tools › Settings…**, *Assistant (AI)*) and see [Connect your AI](/docs/connect).
