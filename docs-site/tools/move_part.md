```text
Move a part about its joint as the person's drag on it does. With no part= it changes nothing and says what moves in the model: what its link's card says (steering, a boom, doors...) with the part to take hold of, and every part on a joint. With part=, its whole rigid body and everything beyond it turn on the joint between it and what stays (a locked part, what stands on the baseplate, else the body with the most parts, or a heavier one standing on the floor): a hinge, a clip on a bar, a turntable, a pin or an axle in a round hole, a ball, a 1x1 round part on one stud. part= a part's number (look_at_model) or a sub-assembly's name. With nothing else it changes nothing and says what would move, on what, and how the model stands. degrees= turns it that far from where it is (a ball: about axis=), along= slides it that many studs along a bar or an axle; it stops where it meets the model, gears it drives turn by their teeth, a joint without friction swings down under its own weight once let go, and the answer says what moved, where its joint then stands from where it was built (twice 20° stands at 40°), and how the model stands then. A part held at both ends (a four-bar, a crank with its rod and piston, a steering's arms) moves as its loop allows, and says so where it locks. spin= lets it go turning that many degrees a second, as a flick does: a wheel, a gear train or a turntable on free bearings coasts and slows against their friction, and the answer says how far it spun and where it stopped. A model turned past its edge falls over and says how it lies, unless it stands on the baseplate's studs. to= [x, y, z] in studs instead draws the part to that point by its whole chain of joints back to what stays, each within its range. One undo step.
```
