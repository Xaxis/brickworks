Features

Everything from the idea to the box of bricks

Built on the exact LDraw geometry rather than an approximation of it, so what you design is what you can build.

The designer

Claude designs it, in real bricks

Say what you want — a sentence or a paragraph — and Claude lays the model out, then goes back over each part of it close up, reaching for the pieces real sets of the kind are built from. You watch it go up in your tab, a brick at a time.

  • Every brick checked against the real 2 LDU lattice before it is placed, so it stands up
  • Furnished rooms, towers, minifigures and LEGO Art, each in one call
  • It looks at its own renders and revises what it built
  • On your Claude plan through the connector, or your own API key

LEGO Art

A picture, laid the way an Art set lays one

Drop in a picture and get a mosaic built as a modern LEGO Art set builds one — not a flat grid of plates — in the colours the parts are really made in. A model like any other: parts list, instructions and all, a panel at a time.

  • 2 × 4 tiles in courses, round and quarter-round accents, stars
  • Raised up to six plates deep, or flat; or classic 1 × 1 plates
  • Claude can plan one too, look at it, and revise it

Hoses and posing

Parts that bend, and models that move

Lay a hose, tube, string or chain along a path between the connectors you click, and reshape it later. Then pose anything that turns on a hinge, a turntable or a pin, and play it back.

  • Pneumatic tube, ribbed and flex-system hose, flexible axle, string, Technic chain and tread
  • A slider for every joint, and keyframes made into an animation
  • A laid hose is counted as the one hose a shop sells, not thirty rings

Studio and your files

Bring your models in, and take them back out

Open a BrickLink Studio .io in its own colours — locked ones too — and save to .io with its build order and a picture. LDraw opens in LDView, LeoCAD, Studio and Mecabricks, and theirs opens here.

  • LDraw .ldr and .mpd in and out; LEGO Digital Designer .lxf in
  • Brickify: an OBJ, STL or glTF sculpt laid in LEGO at the size you pick
  • Models are saved in your browser or on your computer, never on our server

Building instructions

A booklet like the one in the box

Instructions laid out as a LEGO booklet, because you build on a table, not at a screen: landscape pages that print on A4 or Letter, from the toolbar's Instructions button.

  • A parts box with a picture of every part, per step
  • Sub-assemblies and minifigures built in callouts, with an arrow to where they go
  • A cover, the model front and back, and the parts list by colour with element numbers

Studio renders

A picture worth keeping

File, Render… draws the model on a studio sweep under three lights, with soft shadows where it meets the floor — a still, or a turntable that goes all the way round.

  • Quick, Good or Best, previewed before you press Render
  • 720p, 1080p, 4K, square or any size you like
  • On the desktop and in a browser

Share links

A link anyone can open in 3D

File, Share… gives you a brickworks.diy/m/ link. Pasted anywhere, it shows the model's picture and a card; opened, it is the model in 3D, with its steps, its booklet and its parts list.

  • Open in Brickworks copies it into the visitor's own models
  • An embed card for a blog or a forum
  • Take a link down whenever you like

Collection and shops

Build from what you own; buy only what you need

Tell it the sets and parts you own and it says what the open model still needs, what could stand in, and which models you could build — then hands the shortfall to a shop.

  • Sets by number, a Rebrickable or BrickLink file, or your Rebrickable account
  • A BrickLink wanted list or a Rebrickable parts list, by the lot
  • 328 official LDraw models of real sets to rank against what you have

And everything a builder expects

The rest of the toolbox, all of it in the browser.

Build by hand

Point at a face and the part goes against it. Positions are whole numbers on a 2 LDU lattice, so a model saved today loads identically tomorrow.

Every part, findable

All 28,319, searchable as you type by name, number and the sets they came in, previewed in the colour you picked.

Will it stand up?

Live checks for a joint carrying more than its studs hold, a load hanging past its footprint, and a build too tall for its base.

Real connections

Studs, tubes, pin and axle holes, clips on bars, hinges and turntables, read from the primitives each part is drawn with.

Minifigures

A builder with all 4,263 minifigure parts — and prints you draw yourself — that stands figures on two studs in your model.

Prints and stickers

Draw a print, or bring in a picture, for any face of a part, and print a model's sticker sheet at true size.

A part LEGO never made

Make a brick, plate, tile, slope or round part at any size. It clutches like the rest and travels inside any file that uses it.

Watch it go together

A timeline from an empty baseplate to the finished model and back: in build order, in the order it was made, or every change.

Every colour, every finish

All 322 colours in the plastic they come in — chrome, pearl, metallic, glitter, speckle, glow, rubber — and see inside with a cut or x-ray.

How it works

Three steps, and nothing to pay us

Brickworks sells nothing. Claude does the designing, and Anthropic bills it the way you already pay for Claude.

  1. Open the app and sign in

    Your email address is your account: we send a code, there is no password to choose, leak or reset.

  2. Bring your own Claude

    Connect Claude on your Pro or Max plan, or paste an Anthropic API key. Either way, you pay Anthropic as you already do.

  3. Describe it, or build it

    Ask for a model and watch it built, or place parts yourself. Then take away the parts list, the booklet, a render or a link.

Your Claude plan

Claude Pro or Max · no key, nothing to install

  1. In the app, press Connect Claude. claude.ai opens with Brickworks filled in: press Add, then Connect, and Allow when Brickworks asks. Once, for your account — the Claude desktop and mobile apps have it too.
  2. Or in Claude Code, then /mcp and Authenticate: claude mcp add --transport http brickworks https://brickworks.diy/api/mcp
  3. With Brickworks open, ask Claude to “build a small red house in Brickworks”. It builds in your open tab, on any device, and every step shows as it goes.

Counts against your plan’s usage, like any other chat with Claude.

Your own API key

Pay as you go · from the Anthropic Console

  1. Make a key at console.anthropic.com — safest in a workspace of its own, with a spend limit, set to expire.
  2. Paste it into the app. It is checked with Anthropic before it is kept, in your browser or for this visit only.
  3. Describe what you want and press Build it. Choose the model and how hard it thinks.

Measured at the default setting: a small house took 11 minutes and $2.40, a castle 45 minutes and $5–10. Medium effort costs about a third.

Nothing is billed by Brickworks. There is no plan, no credit card and no usage of ours to run out of: building by hand is free, and designing with Claude is between you and Anthropic.

Privacy

What Brickworks keeps, and what it never sees

Your account is your email address, and that is nearly all there is of you here: the address, when you last signed in, which Claude apps you connected, and for a day the network address a sign-in code was asked from, so the form cannot be used to flood an inbox.

Your email address

Your account. You sign in with a code we email you and stay signed in on that browser or computer until you sign out. It is used to sign you in and for nothing else.

Your API key

Kept in your browser, or for this visit only, and sent only to Anthropic. The page’s security policy lets it talk to itself, Anthropic, Wikimedia for reference pictures, Rebrickable, and its own parts store and sign-in service — nothing else.

Your Claude login

Never asked for. Claude signs in to Brickworks — as you, with your email code and your Allow — and holds a Brickworks token, never the other way round. Disconnect Claude under Account ends it everywhere at once.

Your conversations

A small relay carries Claude’s tool calls to your open tab and the answers back, and deletes each message the moment it is answered. Claude’s tokens are kept only as hashes.

Your models

Saved in your browser or on your computer, not on a server. A model is put online only when you share it by link, and taken down when you say.

Every line of it is on GitHub, so none of this has to be taken on trust.

Measured, not approximated

1 LDU = 0.4 mm exactly. Every dimension was read off the part geometry rather than copied from a reference, and the tests assert them, so a library update cannot move them quietly.

FAQ

Questions people ask

And if yours is not here, ask it on GitHub.

Is Brickworks free?

Yes. Building, saving, instructions, renders and share links cost nothing, and there is nothing to subscribe to. Designing with Claude uses your own Claude plan or Anthropic API key, so Anthropic bills that the way it already bills you — never Brickworks.

Do I need a Claude subscription?

No. Everything but the designer works without one: you can build entirely by hand. To have Claude design, connect a Claude Pro or Max plan, or paste an API key from the Anthropic Console.

Does Brickworks see my API key?

No. It stays in your browser — or only for the visit, if you choose — and is sent only to Anthropic. The page's security policy refuses to send anything anywhere but its own short list, so a key has nowhere else to go.

Where are my models saved?

In your browser, or on your computer in the desktop app — not on a server and not with your account. Export them as LDraw .ldr or Studio .io to keep or move. A model goes online only when you share it by link.

Can it open my BrickLink Studio, LDD or LDraw files?

Yes: Studio .io (locked ones too), LEGO Digital Designer .lxf, and LDraw .ldr and .mpd. It saves LDraw and .io, and turns OBJ, STL and glTF models into bricks.

Can I buy the parts to build it?

Export the parts list as a BrickLink wanted list or a Rebrickable parts list, by the lot, the way shops sell them. My collection first takes off what you already own.

What do I need to run it?

A computer with a recent desktop browser — it runs on WebGL 2 and WebAssembly threads — or the desktop app for Linux, macOS and Windows, which carries every part on disk and builds with no network at all.

Is Brickworks made by the LEGO Group?

No. LEGO® is a trademark of the LEGO Group, which does not sponsor, authorize or endorse this site. The parts come from the community's LDraw library, licensed CC BY 4.0, and the catalogue data from Rebrickable.
