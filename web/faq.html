Designing with Claude

Building by hand needs nothing but an account, and there is no AI in the window until you ask for it. To have Claude design, open Brickworks with the assistant on (or turn it on in Settings, Assistant), then bring your own Claude, one of two ways. Brickworks bills nothing.

Your Claude plan

Claude Pro or Max · no key, nothing to install

  1. Open the app with the assistant on and press Connect Claude. claude.ai opens with Brickworks filled in: press Add, then Connect, and Allow when Brickworks asks. Once, for your account; the Claude desktop and mobile apps have it too. Connecting again later, after a Disconnect, is Connect on the Brickworks in Customize › Connectors, which Connect Claude opens for you then.
  2. Or in Claude Code, then /mcp and Authenticate: claude mcp add --transport http brickworks https://brickworks.diy/api/mcp
  3. With Brickworks open and its assistant on, ask Claude to “build a small red house in Brickworks”. It builds in your open tab, on any device, and every step shows as it goes.

Counts against your plan’s usage, like any other chat with Claude.

Your own API key

Pay as you go · from the Anthropic Console

  1. Make a key at console.anthropic.com, safest in a workspace of its own, with a spend limit, set to expire.
  2. Paste it into the app’s assistant. It is checked with Anthropic before it is kept, in your browser or for this visit only.
  3. Describe what you want and press Build it. Choose the model and how hard it thinks.

Measured at the default setting: a small house took 11 minutes and $2.40, a castle 45 minutes and $5–10. Medium effort costs about a third.

Questions people ask

Why Brickworks, when BrickLink Studio exists?

Studio is the community’s standard and does a lot well. Brickworks runs in any browser, a tablet’s or a phone’s too, with nothing to install; weighs balance and joint strength, and lists the weakest joints; turns gear trains at their real ratios; opens and saves Studio .io; exports STL, OBJ and GLB; and builds one model with others, live. See it beside Studio, Mecabricks, LeoCAD and LDCad, with where each of them still leads.

Is Brickworks free?

Yes. Building, saving, building together, instructions, renders and share links cost nothing, and there is nothing to subscribe to. Designing with Claude uses your own Claude plan or Anthropic API key, so Anthropic bills that the way it already bills you, never Brickworks.

How do I sign in?

With a code we email you, or with orangecheck: its own window, with a Bitcoin wallet, email, Google or GitHub. Either way there is no password to keep. The desktop app signs in by the emailed code, and with orangecheck from 0.8.0. More on what each keeps.

Do I need a Claude subscription?

No. Everything but the assistant works without one, and the assistant is off until you turn it on in Settings, Assistant, or open the app with it on: build entirely by hand and there is no AI anywhere in the window. To bring Claude in, turn it on, then connect a Claude Pro or Max plan, or paste an API key from the Anthropic Console.

Does Brickworks see my API key?

No. It stays in your browser, or only for the visit if you choose, and is sent only to Anthropic. The page's security policy refuses to send anything anywhere but its own short list, so a key has nowhere else to go. More on what Brickworks keeps.

Where are my models saved?

In your browser, or on your computer in the desktop app, and kept privately with your account too, so they open in File › Open wherever you sign in. Export them as LDraw .ldr or Studio .io to keep or move. Nobody else sees a model until you share it.

Can I build a model with someone else?

Yes, live. File › Build together… gives a link to your model, and whoever opens it, signed in, builds it with you at the same time in their browser (the desktop app from 0.8.0). Each of you sees the other’s parts go down, move and change colour as it happens, and their pointer with their name; Undo takes back only your own changes, and there is a short chat for people 13 and over. A reload goes back into the session by itself. You end it, and the model stays yours.

What do the buttons on a shared model’s page do?

Build it opens it in 3D: turn it, step through its build, and get its booklet and parts list, with no account. Open in Brickworks copies it into your own models, to change and make yours; share that, and its page shows it as a remix. Under them you can like it, save it to your favourites or a collection, share its link, comment on it or on one of its steps, and say I built it with a photo of your build. More copies the code to embed it in a page of your own, and is where to report it.

What does Tested mean on a model?

That a person can build it. Brickworks tests a model as it is shared: built in its own steps, each part goes on where something holds it and the model stands at every step; every part is in a colour it was made in; and, finished, every part is held by a stud, a joint or what catches it, no two passing into each other, by the parts’ real shapes. A model that is not marked tells its owner why on its page.

Can it open my BrickLink Studio, LDD or LDraw files?

Yes: Studio .io (locked ones too), LEGO® Digital Designer .lxf, and LDraw .ldr and .mpd. It saves LDraw and .io, exports glTF, OBJ and STL, saves a booklet as a PDF or PNG pages, and turns OBJ, STL and glTF models into bricks.

Can I buy the parts to build it?

Export the parts list as a BrickLink wanted list or a Rebrickable parts list, by the lot, the way shops sell them. My collection first takes off what you already own.

It says “Brickworks has been updated”. What do I do?

Press Reload when it suits you. The model in that tab comes back as it is, under its name. Until then the tab keeps working, but it saves nothing to your saved models, so it can never write over what a tab on the new version keeps. Save again after the reload.

Claude can’t see a new Brickworks tool. What now?

Brickworks tells Claude when its tools change, and a Claude app that listens for that picks them up by itself. If yours hasn’t, reload the Brickworks tab first if it says it has been updated. Then start a new conversation. If the tool is still missing, reconnect Brickworks in Claude: Customize › Connectors, select Brickworks, Disconnect, then Connect; in Claude Code, run /mcp, choose brickworks and Reconnect. Not Disconnect Claude in Brickworks: that ends Claude’s connection, and you connect it and allow it again.

Claude asks before every Brickworks tool. Can it stop asking?

Yes, in Claude: Customize › Connectors, select Brickworks, and under Tool permissions choose Always allow for the tools you trust, or press Always allow when Claude asks. Those switches are Claude’s, so Brickworks cannot set them for you. It does tell Claude which of its tools only look and change nothing. Brickworks asks you to Allow once, and not again while Claude stays connected. To connect again after a Disconnect, press Connect on the Brickworks already in your connectors rather than adding it again: adding it again sets up a new one, with its tool permissions to choose again.

What do I need to run it?

A recent browser on a computer, a tablet or a phone (it runs on WebGL 2 and WebAssembly threads). On a tablet or a phone the app is laid out for a finger: one row of tools, and the parts, the colours and the rest in panels beside the model or, on a phone, sheets over it. Or the desktop app for Linux, macOS and Windows, which carries every part on disk and builds with no network at all.

Does it work with a screen reader, or the keyboard alone?

In the desktop app, from 0.8.0. Every button, part, colour and field says its name (“Select, V”, “Brick 2 x 4, 3001”), and what the status line says is read out. F6 (or Tab, with a screen reader running) goes from the model into the toolbar, the parts and the colours; Return takes a part, and Return again puts it down at the middle of the view. Settings has Interface size, 85 to 150%, and Reduce motion. In a browser the app draws its controls itself, where a screen reader cannot reach them; this site’s own pages work with both.

How exact are the parts?

1 LDU is 0.4 mm exactly, and every dimension was read off the part geometry rather than copied from a reference. The tests assert them, so a library update cannot move them quietly.

Sizes are nominal rather than as moulded, which is what makes parts tile exactly instead of leaving a gap that grows along a long wall. The stud height is the LDraw library's own convention, and every part follows it.
